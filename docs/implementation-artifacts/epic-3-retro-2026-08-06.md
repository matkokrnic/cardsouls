## 3. Q1 — Where did E3 spend time that produced no shipped behaviour?

**(a) `3-0d`'s four post-dev-pass fix rounds — the largest single sink in the epic.** After the dev
pass shipped, the story took `R13` (panel control), `R14-R19` (post-review), `R20-R24` (structural),
`R25-R29` (closing verification), then `R30` at close-out. The middle two rounds produced **negative**
net behaviour: `R20` deleted both source scans the story had shipped and the `R14-R17` round had just
*hardened*, replacing them with structural consumption — 817 insertions against 413 deletions of code
written days earlier. `R25-R28` corrected three **false sentences** and deleted one **vacuous
assertion**; behaviour unchanged. The log states the lesson itself at `R20` — "a text scan over source
cannot carry a design invariant, and this project will stop trying to make it" — with the corollary
that a guard evaded twice gets its *mechanism* replaced, not its *pattern* widened. That lesson cost
three review rounds to buy. `3-0d` spent 166 KB of artifact and 1013 log lines on a debug SAVE button,
a reload button, and a `user://` file format.

**(b) The E3 revisit gate — 230 log lines, zero code.** Its output was correct and load-bearing:
`E3-RG/R1`'s mana-scale finding prevented authoring card costs against an 80-mana cap that
contradicted the GDD's ~10; `E3-RG/R9` caught that folding the seed into the hot-reloadable
`BalanceConfig` would be a determinism hole. It also spent a substantial fraction of itself
**re-auditing the project's own base-rate claim** — recounting seven vs. thirteen NOT READY gates,
qualifying which stories had gate sessions at all. That part is bookkeeping about bookkeeping.

**(c) Deferred-flush overhead.** Eleven architecture amendments accumulated across the epic, each
recorded, counted, cross-checked ("the eighth queue member", "item 5 expanded, not a new member",
"explicitly counted against the prior ten by content"), and re-confirmed at the flush by searching the
log for every ruling naming the queue. The architecture doc was **wrong in eleven documented places
for the duration of E3**, and `E3-RG/R8` shows the cost concretely: the doc described
`EconomyEvaluator`, `ResourceGenerationRule` and `CardCastCondition` as already landed, in five
separate places with no "Planned (E3)" qualifier, while **zero of them existed in `src/`**. That had to
be handled as a first-class finding in `3-4`'s Dev Notes rather than read off a correct document.

**(d) NO CHANGE verdicts reached at full ritual cost.** `3-0b` AC8 (feel/timing): NO CHANGE. AC7
(block pop): KEEP. AC12: KEEP `normalize_move_magnitude = true`. AC13: collision height left at 2.0.
Four of thirteen ACs in a seven-pass story resolved to "leave it alone" — correct outcomes, reached
through the same measurement/mutation/golden apparatus as a behavioural change.

**(e) Ritual applied to zero-behaviour passes.** The supersession de-vacuization pass (`SDV/R1-R7`)
repointed six tests and mutation-proved all six with `src/` byte-identical throughout: 34 log lines,
two commits, no behaviour. `3-2` shipped a **comment-only** reciprocal cross-reference in
`economy_evaluator.gd`.

**What this is not.** The 2.7:1 test-to-source ratio is not waste. E3's mutation tables found real
defects — a blind spot in a guard the same story had just written (`3-5b`), two pre-existing
`E1_BALANCE_FIELDS` omissions unaudited for four and eleven stories, and a pre-existing chain-retrigger
defect found as `3-0b` Pass 3b bycatch. **The waste is concentrated in prose about code, not in tests
of code.**

## 4. Q2 — Which installed skills reproduce rituals we run by hand, and what changes in E4?

### 4.1 Correction to the ratified report

The report as first delivered claimed **"E3 ran 0-for-11 on skills."** That was **wrong**, and wrong
for a stated methodological reason worth keeping: it measured **skill names appearing in story prose**,
which is an artifact of what an author chose to write down, not a trace of what ran.

The machine-checkable trace is the **`baseline_commit` front-matter key, written by `gds-dev-story`**.
Measured by content: it is present in **exactly thirteen story files** — twelve in E1 (`1-1`, `1-2`,
`1-3`, `1-3b`, `1-3c`, `1-4`, `1-5`, `1-6`, `1-7`, `1-7b`, `1-8`, `1-9`) and **`3-6`**. It is absent
from `1-10`, all six E2 stories, and ten of the eleven E3 stories. `3-6`'s value,
`c6357be918bbd16ee7ed91879a15d74d2b40803b`, resolves to `docs(decision-log): 3-6 readiness gate
outcome` — the commit immediately preceding its dev pass.

The prose measure missed three files: `1-8`, `1-9`, and `3-6`.

**Corrected finding: E3 ran 1 of 11 stories on the skills. `3-6-card-hud-hand-mana-deck` ran
end-to-end through `gds-create-story`, `gds-dev-story` and `gds-code-review`.** The other ten were
authored, gated, developed, reviewed and closed by hand-written prompt.

The lesson generalises past this one error: **provenance measured from prose measures authorship
habits; provenance measured from a machine-written key measures what ran.** Prefer the key.

### 4.3 The `gds-code-review` trial already happened — `3-6` is the story

The first version of this report recommended adopting `gds-code-review` first and alone "until it
proves itself on one story." That recommendation was already spent: **`3-6` is that story.** Its
outcome, so the recommendation is not re-issued as if untested:

**What it caught — two real patch-level defects, both fixed and shipped** in
`fix(hud): 3-6 review patches` (2 files, +23/-2), recorded in the story's `### Review Findings`
section under the skill's own triage labels:

1. `[Review][Patch]` — the reshuffle timer callback could fire against a **freed `HudRoot`**
   (`on_reshuffle_vulnerable_window_opened`). Fixed with an `is_instance_valid` guard.
2. `[Review][Patch]` — deck/reshuffle labels lacked the overflow protection the card captions already
   had (`_build_deck_indicator`). Fixed with `clip_text` plus an ellipsis trim.

A third finding, `[Review][Defer]` — the hand row hard-coded to four slots with silent truncation —
became `3-6/R8`, the `hand_size <= 4` authoring-audit bound, which **closed a finding that had been
sitting open in `deferred-work.md`**.

**Method note worth keeping (operator-observed):** the review did **not** trust the dev pass's own
mutation claim. It ran an **independent mutation grep** against the shipped tree. Independent
verification of a claim the dev pass made about itself is exactly the property the hand review process
had to be taught repeatedly across `3-0d`'s four rounds.

**Named failure mode (operator-observed): the Acceptance Auditor layer hung at 600 s, and the
acceptance audit had to be done by hand.** Two of three layers returned; the third did not.

**Honest reading of the trial.** The substitution is **proven at patch level and unproven at
acceptance level** — and acceptance is precisely the layer that failed. That matters because E3's
most expensive review failure, `3-0d`'s guard evaded three times across three sequential rounds, was
an *acceptance/structural* failure, not a patch-level one. `3-6` does not yet test the case that hurt
most. The sample is also favourable in a second way: `3-6` is the **smallest artifact in the epic**
(18.4 KB) and a two-pass story. The skill-run story was the easiest story in E3.

### 4.4 What changes if E4 runs on skills plus `_bmad/custom/*.toml`

**Gains.** `gds-code-review`'s layers run in **parallel and independently**. E3's worst failure was
serial: one reviewer iterating on its own prior output, hardening the same wrong pattern twice before
`R20` replaced the mechanism. Three orthogonal reviewers are structurally more likely to reach that
conclusion in one round. `3-6` gives partial evidence for this and a known reliability caveat.

**Losses, unless overridden.** The skills do not produce the numbered-ruling decision log, which is
this project's actual institutional memory — `3-5b` inherited `3-5a`'s ruling text; the E3 close-out
reconstructed an eleven-member queue purely from ruling cross-references. They also carry defaults
that collide with locked repo rules: `gds-retrospective` alone would write into
`docs/implementation-artifacts/`, mutate `sprint-status.yaml` with a status the board's locked
definitions do not have, and stage a dialogue between five invented personas.

**The override seat exists and is empty.** Every skill resolves
`{skill-root}/customize.toml` -> `_bmad/custom/{skill-name}.toml` ->
`_bmad/custom/{skill-name}.user.toml`, deep-merged, with `activation_steps_prepend`/`_append` and
`persistent_facts` (including `file:` globs). That is the seat for this repo's invariants: F1 /
D3(a) / D3(b); the golden-discipline rules (`BC/R3`, `SC/R6`); "every new guard proven falling by
mutation"; restore-from-out-of-repo-copy, never `git checkout --`; "docs and code never share a
commit"; `git add` and `git commit` separate with explicit paths; the `Claude Opus 4.8` trailer
invariant; the `R-D6` live-smoke acceptance; and the locked three-state board. **Both
`_bmad/custom/` files currently contain nothing but comment headers — zero overrides authored.**

**Recommendation for E4.** Author the `_bmad/custom/*.toml` overrides first, then continue
`gds-code-review` — it has one story of evidence and one named reliability defect, not zero. Prove it
on an **acceptance-heavy** story (`4-0-hand-slot-stability` qualifies: it changes state semantics and
moves the golden) with the Acceptance Auditor hang treated as expected and hand-audited on timeout.
Gates and close-outs stay on hand-written prompts until the ruling-log gap is solved by an override,
not by hope.

## 6. Lessons carried forward

1. **A verdict that is always the same is not a signal.** Twenty-two gates, twenty-two NOT READY. Read
   the finding count, not the verdict.
2. **Measure provenance from machine-written keys, not prose.** §4.1 — this retrospective's own
   first draft got the headline skills finding wrong by measuring what authors wrote down.
3. **A guard evaded twice gets its mechanism replaced, not its pattern widened** (`3-0d/R20`),
   and an adversarial pass must declare its failure criterion in advance (`3-0d`, close-out).
4. **Deferring documentation to a single flush leaves the document wrong for the whole epic**, and the
   flush itself can drop riders — it dropped two (§1.3).
5. **A decision parked at a venue that never convenes stays parked.** Decision (c) waited two epics for
   an E2 retrospective that was never held.
