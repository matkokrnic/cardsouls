## 9. Lessons carried forward

1. **A decision named in a fence list is not owned.** `E4-P/R11` re-fenced (a), (b), (e) and gave none
   an owner; (b) has now skipped two epics the way (c) did. Only (f) discharged — and only because its
   fence carried a named mechanism for adoption.
2. **Gate rounds are the E4-shaped sink, not prose.** Every docs ratio improved on E3 while eleven
   docs-only gate rounds produced zero commits, four of them deleting specification an earlier round
   added. Specification inflation is guard-pattern widening (`3-0d/R20`) one layer up.
3. **A convention nothing greps is not enforced.** The layer-completion line held in 4 of 15 stories;
   its counter went unmaintained for nine. A rule that lives in chat leaves no evidence a retrospective
   can read.
4. **Report-only, then ratify, then write** — two for two on catching prompt-vs-repo divergence a
   single pass would have recorded as fact, against an E3 close-out that dropped two riders.
5. **Read-only subagents prevent a class that has not yet occurred.** Every incident E4 actually had
   survives the rule; what catches those is the evidence audit, which is why it became an acceptance
   criterion rather than a checklist item.
6. **A rule whose enforcement costs the operator more than the failure it prevents is rejected** — the
   yardstick that killed the per-story architecture-line check and the `story_note` length cap, and
   kept `E4-R/R4` and `E4-R/R7`, which cost one grep each.
