CARDSOULS

Technical Design Document — Demo Scope

Version 0.3 — Draft

## 1. Project Overview

CardSouls is a 1v1 online PvP game combining real-time souls-like melee combat with a real-time card economy. The player directly controls their hero in third-person combat while simultaneously managing a hand of cards that summon units, cast spells, and trigger special attacks. Both layers — manual combat and card economy — must be equally relevant to winning.

Engine: Unreal Engine 5

Primary language: C++

Multiplayer: Online P2P, 2 players, no teams

Match format: Best of 3 rounds

## 2. Camera & Perspective

Third-person perspective, camera pulled back slightly further than Elden Ring's default — player character fully visible with some terrain context. Fixed camera distance (no zoom). Camera follows the local player's hero. Both players visible on screen when in proximity; no minimap required for demo scope.

## 3. Arena / Map

Flat arena — no elevation, no obstacles, no corridors. Boundary walls or invisible kill plane to prevent infinite kiting. Exact dimensions TBD during playtesting; recommended starting point is roughly 40x40 meters. No pre-placed objects — all units, totems, and wards are introduced exclusively through cards.

> **Note:** Arena size directly affects how oppressive ranged minions and totems feel. Size should be iterated on early.

## 4. Hero

### 4.1 Stats

| Stat | Design Note |
| --- | --- |
| HP | Absolute value TBD — define relative to spell/minion damage during balance pass. No passive regeneration. Healing only via cards (spells or minions with heal ability). |
| Stamina | Regenerates automatically over time (Souls-style). Consumed by: Roll, Deflect, initiating Unblockable attacks, and defending against Unblockable attacks. If stamina reaches zero, player cannot roll, deflect, or use Unblockable card modes until it recovers. |
| Mana | Two sources: (1) slow passive auto-regen baseline, (2) generated by landing ordinary attacks on the opponent — aggressiveness directly rewards card economy. Mana Accelerators (summoned via cards) add a third sustained ramp layer. Used to play Basic card mode and Pitch Effect card mode. |

### 4.2 Core Actions

- Basic Attack — standard melee attack chain, no resource cost
- Block / Deflect — hold to block, precise timing window triggers a deflect (parry). Costs stamina.
- Roll (Dodge) — i-frame dodge roll in movement direction. Costs stamina.
- Unblockable Initiation — triggered via card (see Section 6). Costs stamina.
- Unblockable Defense — triggered via card (see Section 6). Costs stamina. Instant response, no chargeup.

## 5. Card System

### 5.1 Deck

- Deck size: 20 cards
- Maximum copies of any single card: 2-3 (exact cap configurable per card in data)
- Sideboard: 6 cards (8 TBD) — see Section 9
- Deck is constructed before the match in a pre-game lobby alongside equipment selection
- On match start: player draws 4 cards (full starting hand)
- On card played: draw 1 replacement card immediately (or after ~1 second delay — tune during playtesting)
- On deck exhausted: deck reshuffles automatically. Brief vulnerable window during reshuffle (duration TBD — suggested 1.5-2 seconds, flagged visually to both players)

### 5.2 Hand

- Hand size: 4 cards at all times
- Cards are visible to the local player only, except cards placed in the Pitch Zone (see 5.5)

### 5.3 Card Types

| Type | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Spell | Active effect cast from the hero — damage, debuffs, area denial, utility. Elden Ring-style ability attacks in feel. Instant or short-animation cast. |
| Minion | Summons a unit that acts autonomously (Clash Royale / TFT AI behavior). Attacks enemy minions and/or the enemy hero depending on its priority type (see 5.4). |
| Totem / Ward | Static structure placed on the arena floor. Three subtypes: (a) Combat Totem — has a defined attack range, fires at enemies within range, can be destroyed. (b) Mana Accelerator — adds a sustained passive mana regen layer on top of auto-regen and melee-generated mana; provides steady economy rather than burst, can be destroyed. (c) Stamina Accelerator — increases stamina regen rate while alive, enabling more frequent rolls, deflects, and Unblockable usage; can be destroyed. All totems are intentionally small and unimposing visually — more like wardstones than towers. |

### 5.4 Minion AI Priority Types

Each minion card defines a priority type. Examples for demo:

- Standard — targets nearest enemy unit, will hit hero if no units present
- Hero-Seeker — always targets enemy hero, ignores enemy units
- Tank — maximum threat generation; all enemy minions redirect aggro to this unit
- Bomber / AoE — prioritizes clumps of units

> **Note:** Priority types are data-defined per card, not hardcoded. New types added without code changes.

### 5.5 Four Play Modes per Card

Every card has exactly four ways to be played. The player selects the mode at time of play:

| Mode | Name | Effect | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Basic | Summons a minion / totem / mana accelerator, or casts a lesser spell. Defined per card. | Mana (amount defined per card) |
| 2 | Unblockable Initiation | Hero performs the Unblockable attack corresponding to this card's color. See Section 6. | Stamina only. No mana cost. |
| 3 | Unblockable Defense | Hero performs the defensive counter corresponding to this card's color. See Section 6. | Stamina only. No mana cost. |
| 4 | Pitch Effect | Powerful effect declared on the card. Activated from the Pitch Zone. See Section 5.6. | Mana (higher than Basic) + Orbs (color and quantity defined per card). Orb pool resets to 0 on activation. |

> **Note:** Modes 2 and 3 consume the card from hand when used. The card goes to discard and triggers a draw.

### 5.6 Pitch Zone

The Pitch Zone is a single card slot in the UI (positioned near the hand display). It functions as a publicly visible held card with a countdown timer.

- Player drags a card from hand to the Pitch Zone to stage it
- The staged card and its Pitch Effect cost (mana + required orbs) become visible to the opponent
- A visible countdown timer starts immediately on both players screens when a card is staged — 20 or 30 seconds TBD during playtesting
- If the timer expires before activation, the card fizzles — it is discarded and a new card is drawn. Orbs and mana already accumulated are NOT refunded.
- Card can also leave the Pitch Zone via: (a) player manually activates it when cost is met, (b) player manually cancels and returns it to hand, or (c) a specific opponent card effect removes it (future content)
- Only one card can be in the Pitch Zone at a time
- Staging a card does NOT reduce hand size below 4 — the staged card counts as still in hand until activated or cancelled

> **Note:** The fizzle timer creates a hard deadline for both players — the threatening player cannot park a card indefinitely while farming orbs, and the defending player knows exactly how long they must survive the threat.

> **Note:** 20s vs 30s is a significant feel difference — 20s is aggressive and pressured, 30s is more strategic. Tune during playtesting.

## 6. Mana Economy

Mana has three distinct sources which stack and complement different playstyles:

| Source | Description | Playstyle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Passive auto-regen | Slow baseline tick, always active regardless of combat state. Provides a minimum floor of mana generation. | Universal — both players benefit equally. |
| Melee hit generation | Landing ordinary attacks on the opponent generates mana in bursts. Aggressiveness directly rewards card economy — the player winning in melee has more resources to spend on cards. | Aggressive / in-your-face playstyle. |
| Mana Accelerator (totem) | Summoned via card. Adds a sustained passive regen layer on top of auto-regen. Unlike melee bursts, this is continuous and does not require staying in combat range. Can be destroyed by opponent. | Economy / ramp playstyle — invest early to outscale later. |

> **Note:** Ordinary attacks deal moderate but non-trivial damage (suggested ~5-8% HP per hit) — enough that melee cannot be ignored, not enough to win without cards.

> **Note:** Mana Accelerators complement melee-generated mana rather than replacing it — they provide steady sustained flow where melee provides spikes. A player with accelerators has consistent economy; a player without must stay aggressive to keep up.

## 7. Unblockable Attack System

### 7.1 Overview

Inspired by Sekiro's Perilous Attack system. Each card has a color (Red, Blue, or Green) which determines which Unblockable attack it can initiate and which it can defend against. This creates a rock-paper-scissors layer on top of standard melee combat.

### 7.2 Attack & Counter Table

| Color | Attack Name | Counter Name | Counter Logic |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Red | Swipe / Headstomp — wide horizontal sweep or downward stomp | Headstomp — jump over the sweep and stomp on attacker | Attacker is below you mid-swing — exploit the dead angle above. |
| Blue | Jump Attack — leaping overhead strike | Pseudo-Suriken — midair interception projectile | Attacker is committed and airborne — intercept before they land. |
| Green | Thrust Attack — lunging stab / thrust | Pseudo-Mikirikaeshi — step inside attacker's effective range | Thrust is only effective at distance — close the gap to nullify it. |

### 7.3 Initiation

- Player selects Mode 2 on a card of the desired color
- Hero begins a short chargeup animation before the attack lands
- Audio and visual telegraph fires during chargeup — opponent must recognize the attack type and respond with the correct color defense
- Chargeup has relatively generous auto-aim; landing the attack does not require precise manual aim
- Costs stamina — if stamina is insufficient, Mode 2 is greyed out on the card

### 7.4 Defense

- Player selects Mode 3 on a card matching the incoming attack's color
- Defense is instant on input — no chargeup
- Must be performed during the attacker's chargeup window
- Mismatched color defense: fails (attack lands normally)
- No defense input: attack lands normally

### 7.5 Outcomes

| Scenario | Attacker Result | Defender Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Attack lands (no defense / wrong color) | Deals fixed damage. Gains 1 Orb of attack color. | Takes damage. |
| Attack countered (correct color defense) | Stunned for ~1 second (cannot perform any action). | No damage taken. No orb gained. |

> **Note:** Damage value for Unblockable attacks is fixed per attack type, not per card. Define as a single data value per color in balance config.

## 8. Orb Resource

### 8.1 Acquisition

Orbs are gained exclusively by landing a successful Unblockable attack (attack not countered). The attacker gains 1 Orb of the attack's color (Red, Blue, or Green).

### 8.2 Storage

- No maximum orb cap — player can accumulate orbs freely
- Orb counts are displayed per color in the HUD
- On Pitch Effect activation: orb pool for all colors resets to 0

> **Note:** The all-color reset on Pitch activation is intentional — it prevents stockpiling and forces the decision of when to spend.

### 8.3 Usage

Orbs are spent exclusively to activate a card's Pitch Effect (Mode 4). Each card's Pitch Effect specifies its own required orb combination (e.g., '2 Green', '1 Red + 1 Blue'). No other use for orbs exists.

## 9. Example Card — Imp Summoner

The following card illustrates all four modes in practice:

| Mode | Name | Effect | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Basic | Summon 1 Imp minion. Standard AI priority — targets nearest enemy. | 3 Mana |
| 2 | Unblockable Init. | Hero performs Red Unblockable attack (Swipe/Headstomp). | Stamina |
| 3 | Unblockable Def. | Hero performs Red counter (Headstomp defense). | Stamina |
| 4 | Pitch Effect — Hellburst | All currently alive Imp minions (owned by this player) simultaneously explode, each dealing AoE damage centered on their position. | 5 Mana + 2 Green Orbs. All orbs reset to 0 on activation. |

## 10. Match Format — Best of 3

Matches are played as Best of 3 rounds. First player to win 2 rounds wins the match.

### 11.1 Sideboard

- Each player builds a sideboard of 6 cards (8 TBD) in addition to their 20-card main deck
- Between rounds, player may swap any number of main deck cards with sideboard cards — no swap limit
- Sideboard allows tactical adaptation after seeing opponent's strategy in round 1
- Swapped cards return to sideboard for subsequent rounds — sideboard is not consumed

> **Note:** 6 vs 8 sideboard size TBD during playtesting. 6 is tighter and more decisive; 8 gives more flexibility.

## 11. Equipment

Each player equips 4 equipment pieces before the match. Equipment is visually represented on the hero (Elden Ring style — visible armor/weapon slots). Equipment is selected in the pre-match lobby alongside deck building and cannot be changed for the duration of the match, including between rounds in a Best of 3.

### 11.1 Design Reference

Equipment functions similarly to Flash and Blood equipment — each piece provides a permanent passive effect that modifies hero behavior or thresholds throughout the match. Equipment is not consumed and does not interact with the card economy directly.

### 11.2 Equipment Slots

| Slot | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Head | Helmet / headgear. Visible on hero. |
| Chest | Armor. Visible on hero. |
| Arms / Hands | Gauntlets / bracers. Visible on hero. |
| Legs / Feet | Greaves / boots. Visible on hero. |

> **Note:** Exact slot names and visual representation TBD — align with chosen hero art direction.

### 11.3 Passive Effect Examples

Equipment passives are data-defined per item. Examples of passive types:

- Combat timing — e.g. slightly faster chargeup on Red Unblockable attack
- Threshold-based — e.g. while 5+ friendly totems/wards are alive, incoming damage reduced by X%
- Cooldown immunity — e.g. every 45 seconds, one incoming stun has no effect
- Resource modifiers — e.g. +1 starting orb of a chosen color, or slightly increased mana regen
- Stat adjustments — e.g. increased stamina pool, faster stamina regen

> **Note:** Equipment passive values are balance-critical. Define as data (not hardcoded) so they can be tuned without recompilation.

## 12. Match Flow Summary

Complete sequence of a match for implementation reference:

- Phase 1 — Pre-match lobby: Player selects: 20-card main deck (max 2-3 copies per card), 6-card sideboard, 4 equipment pieces. All selections locked on match start.
- Phase 2 — Round: Both players draw 4 cards, full starting hand. Real-time play begins — combat, cards, unblockables, pitch zone all active simultaneously. Round ends when one player's HP reaches 0.
- Phase 3 — Between rounds: Both players may swap main deck cards with sideboard freely. Equipment unchanged. No time limit defined — TBD.
- Phase 4 — Match end: First to 2 round wins takes the match.

## 13. UI / HUD Layout

Minimum required HUD elements for demo:

- Own HP bar — prominent, top or bottom of screen
- Enemy HP bar — visible, slightly less prominent
- Stamina bar — near own HP
- Mana bar + mana regen tick indicator
- Orb counters — 3 color-coded values (Red / Blue / Green orb count)
- Hand display — 4 card slots, bottom center. Each card shows: card name, Basic cost (mana), color indicator, Pitch Effect cost preview
- Pitch Zone — 1 card slot adjacent to hand. Clearly distinct visual state. Visible to opponent (server communicates Pitch Zone contents).
- Deck counter — remaining cards in deck
- Reshuffle indicator — clear visual when reshuffle is occurring
- Round counter — current round in Best of 3 (e.g. Round 1 / 2 / 3)

> **Note:** Opponent's hand contents are NOT visible. Only the Pitch Zone card is shared.

## 14. Networking

- Online P2P, 2 players
- Authoritative game state: recommend server-authoritative model even for P2P to prevent desync on Unblockable timing — implement as listen-server or dedicated server for demo
- Critical sync points: Unblockable initiation chargeup start, Unblockable defense input, card play events, Pitch Zone changes, unit spawn/death, HP changes
- Latency tolerance: Unblockable system is timing-sensitive — add server-side leniency window (~100-150ms) for defense inputs

> **Note:** Exact networking architecture (P2P vs listen-server) TBD — flag for technical review. Unblockable timing correctness under latency is the highest networking risk.

## 15. Out of Scope for Demo

The following are noted design ideas but explicitly excluded from demo implementation:

- Online multiplayer — demo uses local PvP or single-player vs AI
- Sideboard and Best of 3 format — demo uses single round
- Equipment visual representation on hero — use placeholder stats only
- Card effects that remove opponent's Pitch Zone card
- Minions/spells that heal the hero
- More than one arena layout
- Spectator mode
- Progression system, unlocks, or meta-game
